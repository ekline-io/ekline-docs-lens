# What Docs Lens is

Docs Lens is a free educational tool. We built it as a contribution to
the documentation and technical writing community. EkLine maintains
it. It is not a sales funnel.

The audience is docs owners, DevRel, and technical writers. They want
to understand how AI agents read their documentation. They want to fix
what is broken themselves.

## Primary goal

Help the docs and TW community see, understand, and fix how AI agents
read their websites and knowledge bases. We want their content to work
as well for agent readers as it does for humans. Agent readers means
Claude Code, Cursor, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and the rest.

A good session for a visitor looks like this:

1. They paste a docs URL. They get an honest, traceable read in under
   a minute.
2. They walk away knowing what is broken for agents, why it matters,
   and which agents it affects.
3. They leave with a copyable fix they can apply themselves. No
   consultant required.

## Secondary impact

People learn about EkLine and what we do. This is a side effect of a
useful, trusted tool. It is not the reason the tool exists.

We earn awareness by being helpful, not by gating value. If a change
trades community trust for short-term EkLine pipeline, it is the wrong
change.

## What this means for the product

- No lead capture. No signups. No gated reports. Everything visible
  without an email. Do not add forms, modals, or "talk to sales" CTAs.
- No EkLine sales CTAs in the product flow. EkLine attribution belongs
  in the footer and the "built by" line. Not in verdict banners,
  results pages, or post-scan prompts. The bar is: would the TW
  community trust this if a competitor built it?
- The fix is the deliverable. Findings should empower the user. Use
  the agent-fix prompt, methodology pages, and linked references. Do
  not withhold detail to drive contact.
- Substance over score. Every number ties to a real HTTP response or
  Playwright snapshot. No LLMs in the scoring path. Do not add
  heuristics a skeptic cannot re-run.
- Tone is peer to peer. Write as a fellow practitioner sharing
  findings. Do not write as a marketer chasing intent.
- Community signals matter. Keep open source, methodology
  transparency, and attribution to prior art prominent. Prior art
  includes Fern's agent-score and Cloudflare's isitagentready.

When a UX or copy decision could help the reader or capture the
reader, choose helping every time.

## Voice and language

Don't use SaaS marketing tells. Even the absence-of-sales framing is
sales framing. A community tool doesn't need to claim it's free, any
more than a Wikipedia article does. Just describe what the tool does
and what we found.

Avoid these words and phrases:

- "Free" or "free audit." We don't sell an audit, so calling this one
  free implies there is a paid version somewhere. There isn't.
- "No signup" or "no signup required." This frames the tool against
  SaaS patterns the reader didn't bring up.
- "Audit," "report," "platform," "solution." Service-business
  language. Use "check," "review," "tool" instead.
- "Get started," "try it now," "see for yourself." Sales CTAs.
- "Insights," "actionable," "unlock," "discover." Marketing fillers.
- "Talk to sales," "request a demo," "book a call." Obvious. Never.

Prefer plain, declarative language:

- "Paste a docs URL. We'll show you what three reader profiles get."
- "Here's what we found."
- "Here's a prompt that fixes these issues."
- "Methodology."

The voice is a peer who built a useful thing and is sharing it with
colleagues. Not a vendor demonstrating value.
